# Illustrated tabletop

The owner approved the illustrated tabletop direction on October 3, 2026: an immersive colored map, integrated lettering and floor numbering, natural visible connections, tactile components, clear portals and passages, and restrained charcoal/brass controls. Animations remain deferred. The production map uses a flatter overhead view so the illustrated floors can align with the physical layout without perspective ambiguity.

`boardLayout.ts` places the 29 floor anchors on a square 1000-unit canvas. The anchors follow a manual perspective-normalized study of the owner's private photograph; they preserve relative placement rather than copying the printed illustration. Decorative buildings are original generic fantasy landmarks. The checked geometry is an approximation of the photographed floor positions, not a claim of pixel-identical publisher artwork.

Authored waypoints shape the 28 accepted ordinary edges into streets, corridors, harbor bridges and forest trails. They neither add an edge nor authorize movement. Public tests check bounds, distinct hit targets, route endpoints, unrelated-node intersections and neutral junctions. The conservative city paths remain 3–2–4 and 5–4–7; unverified shortcuts remain unimplemented.

`GameBoard` layers an original locally bundled painting, subtle road traces, floor states, native location buttons, lettering and observed pieces. Every location has its complete live name on a cream caption following the lower floor rim, outside the piece area. Portal captions use a muted violet tint. The decorative painting contains no lettering. Each number sits in a small brass floor marker. Full names and observed contents remain available through accessible button labels. Hidden Lair identities never enter the board renderer.

Violet rings identify all four teleport portals. Amber letters identify both endpoints of each secret passage. The printed A/B pair identities are stable under edge reordering or reversal; other imported pairs receive distinct labels. Virtual route traces appear only during a Move from an incident location; they do not read as ordinary streets. Only engine-projected legal destinations become actionable, and the session revalidates every submitted move. Wizard Monster destinations have a separate amber outline without becoming Move targets.

The painting contains ordinary roads, so `hasIllustratedLayout` requires the exact accepted 28 ordinary edges, two passages and six teleport links, independent of their order or direction. An imported graph with different edges uses code-rendered terrain and its own routes, even when it has the same 29 location IDs. Smaller synthetic boards retain the bounded grid fallback. [Asset provenance and publication notice](../src/ui/assets/README.md) records the original guide, prompt and local runtime boundary.

Phase, Hero and Monster status share the top bar on larger windows. The compact action tray and independently toggled information panels preserve the board's remaining height. Panels stay mounted when moved or closed, preserving drafts, choices, Perk disclosures and reading position. Narrow screens stack panels below the fitted board. Native focus rings, keyboard activation, visible unavailable reasons, saved roll arithmetic and persistent required-choice controls remain part of the existing interaction contract.

This design pass changes presentation and geometry only. Engine decisions, data interpretations, command revisions, saves and replay formats remain unchanged. Full human game acceptance is still tracked in the [owner playtest checklist](Playtest-Checklist.md).

## Accessible physical pieces and decisions

The October 7 approved refinements are implemented with original decorative SVG standees, miniatures, octagonal tokens, item illustrations, and dice. Heroes have blue bases, Citizens gray bases, and the two Monsters distinct silhouettes with black bases. One Item bag represents each location's supply; its × quantity badge counts pieces, while inventory and inspection label Item Strength explicitly. Citizen and Item groups expand into named contents in the inspector, including Citizens' safe destinations. Unrevealed Lairs show only their back and presence; neither the board nor inspector receives their identity.

Activating a floor normally opens a read-only cream inspector. Inspection and information-panel toggles do not submit a game command or clear an unfinished action. Locations provides the complete named list with large native controls, including when the fitted map becomes too small to read comfortably. Move offers both the existing immediate highlighted-board activation and a named destination selector with explicit confirmation. Wizard Hero destination choices share a draft between the map and native selector; map selection focuses Confirm and does not move the Hero. Required response completion focuses Latest result. Ordinary actions and End Hero Phase remain locked throughout a required choice.

Locations and Decks & progress join Inventory, Monsters, Event log and Latest result as independent panels. Closing an information panel restores focus to its invoking control; closing inspection returns to its floor or named-list row. Status and action budget stay visible when the tray is collapsed. Panel bodies and lists scroll independently, controls have at least 44px height, and panel text uses scalable sizing. Narrow screens stack the panels; the map remains a fitted spatial overview, with named selectors available for small targets. Keyboard, 200% text and 320 CSS-pixel checks complement, rather than replace, manual VoiceOver and owner usability acceptance.

Beholder decisions show the observed target, dice faces, remaining POW/HIT count and POW-first order. Slowing decisions preview both Item-discard and accept-penalty allowances using the saved interpretation's stacking or legacy cap; an already skipped upcoming phase is labeled explicitly. All imported eye ranges, effects and damaged/intact status remain public reference information. Hero special results show the die, uncapped total, effective 1–20 value, effect and matched range. No presentation control changes rules, draw order, save format or replay.

## Website audit corrections

The October 7 website audit reproduced keyboard focus loss after ordinary actions, offscreen required decisions at enlarged text, inaccessible scroll regions, and unreadably small bodies when many panels were open. Action cancellation now returns to its card; confirmed Move focuses the destination, other actions focus their visible result, and phase end retains its control or terminal outcome. Required choice and Review action explicitly return focus to the decision or action without clearing its draft. Closing information panels also scrolls the invoking control into view. Unavailable action cards have associated descriptions that include their reason.

All panel elements stay under one stable scrolling layer, while the fitted board occupies a separate center column above it. Minimum reading rows allow many panels to scroll instead of collapsing their contents; the native scrollbar has a reserved gutter. Panel bodies, action details, roll details, attack summaries and choice options support keyboard scrolling. Short desktop windows and increased user text allow vertical page scrolling, preserving decision space. Normal desktop windows retain the fitted table. Panel text uses scalable sizes; the passage-key letter has stronger contrast. Dice/results use named groups rather than repeated navigation landmarks.

These changes preserve rules, saves, board anchors, hidden information and mounted drafts/disclosures. Automated checks are recorded in Verification; actual VoiceOver use and owner play acceptance remain manual.

## Remembered layout and recovery feedback

The solo table remembers optional panels, panel sides, contextual decision side and tray collapse across adventures on this browser origin. **Reset layout** gives a short status announcement and restores defaults without clearing an action draft or required response. Inspector contents are ephemeral even when its side is remembered. Restored information panels return focus to their native navigation buttons when closed. Preference storage failure leaves the table usable.

Operation failures focus a centered visible alert with readable guidance. Import errors provide a direct return to the matching file input; valid imported games that cannot be saved retain the same setup for retry. Save progress/success has its own polite status without nested alerts. Explicit recovery clears stale action intentions and focuses the restored choice or save status, while retry keeps the existing session and authoritative result.

WebKit coverage exercises the same public actions and production recovery as Chromium. Native floor buttons use static grid overlays within SVG foreignObject for floor numbers, badges and piece counters. Piece SVGs omit the CSS drop shadows that WebKit painted at unrelated coordinates, and decorative layers cannot intercept clicks. Floor-number/count geometry and inspection of every illustrated floor are checked in both engines; an isolated prepared-board screenshot verifies actual painted positions. This preserves the illustrated map and original pieces rather than replacing the board for a different browser.
